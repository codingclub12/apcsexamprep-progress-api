# 2026-09-03, claude-code: the mojibake guard caught the second-order bug, not the bug

## What prompted it

A review of the Stage 0/Stage 1 session handoffs found that the mojibake rule in
those documents was itself mojibaked: it told a future session to reject two
literal patterns, and both were the DOUBLE corrupted form. The failure reported
on the live page is the single corrupted form and contains neither pattern. The
review's conclusion was that T-1.2 would ship a hollow validator.

That was correct, and it understated the problem. The same inversion was already
shipped and running.

## What was actually wrong

`smoke/encoding-guard.js` gates every pull request. It reversed candidate
sequences through latin-1 only, on a recorded argument that "latin-1 covers
every byte value, which is the strictly broader case". That is true decoding and
false encoding: going from characters back to bytes, latin-1 cannot express the
27 cp1252 characters in 0x80-0x9F, which is exactly what a corrupted bullet,
dash, curly quote or emoji becomes. It also tried sequence widths 3 and 2 only,
so no 4 byte character was visible to it in either flavour, and every emoji is
4 bytes.

Measured against 12 known cases before the change, it caught 5. It caught the
double corrupted cp1252 form and let the single corrupted form through, which is
the inversion the handoff had, one layer down. Its self-test was seven pasted
latin-1 strings, so it was green either way, and a mutation run against it would
also have been green, because the mutation would have injected the flavour the
suite already tested.

It reported this repository clean. It was not clean:

    tools/ap-cyber-ced/CED-UNIT1-EXTRACT.txt   65 characters, depth 1, latin-1
    lib/command-hazards.js                      1, a corrupted emoji, in the
                                                hazard note that TELLS agents
                                                what mojibake looks like
    smoke/site-crawl.js                         2, pasted test fixtures
    tools/ap-cyber-ced/verify_import.py         3, a pasted pattern list under a
                                                comment claiming it was escaped

`lib/site-crawl.js` had independently found HALF of this and fixed it locally:
its comment works out correctly that the cp1252 flavour is what appears on a
rendered page and that latin-1 alone is blind to it. The finding never travelled
back to the repository guard, and the crawler kept two limits its comment never
mentioned and no test covered. Measured before the change: 6 of 16 known cases
missed, all six emoji, in both flavours. The live page that prompted this had a
corrupted emoji on it, so the nightly crawl was structurally incapable of
reporting the thing it watches for, while reporting the page clean.

## What changed

- `lib/mojibake.js`, new, the one detector. Structural rather than a pattern
  list: anchors on the whole lead class U+00C2-U+00F4, derives the sequence
  width from the lead byte, reverses through both codecs. Pure ASCII, so it is
  scanned by the check it implements.
- `smoke/encoding-guard.js` rebuilt on it. Fixtures are GENERATED by a damage
  simulator over a 30 character corpus at two depths in two flavours, 120 cases,
  each of which must be detected AND exactly recovered. 45 assertions.
- `lib/site-crawl.js` delegates instead of carrying a copy. 0 of 16 missed now.
- `tools/ap-cyber-ced/mojibake.py`, a port, replacing the four literal patterns
  in `validate_csv.py` and `verify_import.py`. `validate_csv.py` gates CED sheets
  into the live store and its old list could not match a corrupted emoji at all.
- `smoke/mojibake-parity.js` runs both languages over one corpus and fails if
  they disagree on index, character, codec or width. It fails rather than skips
  when python3 is missing.
- `scripts/mojibake-rederive.js`, the independent second implementation.
- The 65 characters in the CED Unit 1 extract repaired, and the corrupted hazard
  note rewritten to name the characters rather than paste them.
- `CLAUDE.md`: the rule, stated correctly, pointing at the module.

## Two things I got wrong, and how they were caught

**A false claim in a comment, in the module written to stop false claims in
comments.** The first draft said cp1252's reverse map was a strict superset of
latin-1's and that cp1252 alone would detect everything. It is not and it would
not: the two are disjoint on exactly 27 code points each way. Dropping latin-1
on that reasoning would have gone blind to every C1 control, which is what the
entire latin-1 flavour is built from. The suite now asserts 27 and 27.

**The rederive was wrong before the detector was.** On its first run it found 25
hits against the detector's 65 on the real corrupted CED file. The cause is
worth carrying: `new TextDecoder('iso-8859-1')` is NOT latin-1. The WHATWG
Encoding Standard makes that label an alias for windows-1252, so both derived
tables were the same codec, the latin-1 runs broke at every C1 control, and 40
three-byte sequences went missing. Node's `Buffer.toString('latin1')` is true
ISO-8859-1. The script now asserts the two tables differ in both directions and
says what it means if they do not.

## Two guards deleted for being unkillable

Mutation testing found two checks in the detector that no mutation could kill: a
re-encode identity test and the continuation-byte rule. Checked exhaustively
over every 2 and 3 byte sequence with a legal lead, neither changed the verdict
once.

The re-encode check was deleted. Node's decoder already rejects overlong forms
and surrogates, so the check was decoration; the dependency on the decoder is
now asserted directly in the suite over 1,048,576 sequences, so if a future Node
loosens it the suite goes red and the check comes back.

The continuation-byte rule was KEPT and relabelled a fast path, because it is
worth 3.2x on lead-dense text (13ms against 42ms over 288K characters of
accented prose) and `lib/site-crawl.js` runs it over page bodies reaching 270KB.
Its mutation survives by design. That is written in the code and asserted in the
suite, so the next session to mutation test this does not find a surviving
mutation, conclude the guard is hollow, and delete a speedup.

## What was learned

A guard that catches the second-order form of a bug and misses the first-order
form is worse than no guard, because it reports clean. Both of the ones found
today had that shape, and in both cases the reasoning that produced it is
written in a confident comment right above the code. The comment is not the
problem; the problem is that nothing in the loop ever checked whether the
comment was true. Every claim in this change that could be turned into an
assertion was.
